AI Audit: find out what's worth automating first

The short answer

The Embrose AI AI Audit is a $1,500, fixed-price review of how your business runs, done on-site or remotely by Collin Hill, an AI consultant based in Franklin, Indiana. You get a prioritized list of where AI can save your team time, tool recommendations and a written roadmap. Why start with an audit

Most small businesses I talk to know AI could help, but not where to start or what's hype. Buying tools first usually means paying for subscriptions nobody uses. The audit flips that order: we look at the actual work first (the emails, the data entry, the follow-ups, the copy-and-paste between systems) and only then decide what, if anything, is worth automating.

Sometimes the honest answer is "not much yet," and that's a useful answer too.

How it works

  1. Free 30-minute intro call. We talk through how your business runs and where the time goes. If an audit isn't the right fit, I'll say so.
  2. Workflow review. I sit with you and the people who do the work, in person within about an hour of Franklin or over video, and map the repetitive computer tasks step by step: who does them, how often, in which tools.
  3. Opportunity ranking. I rank each opportunity by how much time it could free up, how hard it is to build, and how much risk it carries (customer-facing messages and money get extra care).
  4. Written roadmap and walkthrough. You get the roadmap in writing, and we go through it together so you can decide what to do next: build it with me, build it yourself, or do nothing.

What you get

  • A review of your workflows, on-site or remote
  • A prioritized list of AI opportunities, specific to your business
  • Tool recommendations (including when the tools you already pay for are enough)
  • A written roadmap with suggested order, rough scope and what stays human
  • A clear view of where data would go, and whether a private/local AI setup makes sense for you

Price: $1,500, fixed. No hourly surprises.

What happens after the audit

You own the roadmap and you're under no obligation. If you want help building, the usual next steps are:

  • Setup Sprint, from $3,500: build and launch 1–2 high-value automations in the tools you already use, with team training and documentation.
  • Process Implementation, from $7,500: end-to-end redesign of a core process, with AI agents and integrations built and tested.
  • Embrose Crew 30-Day Pilot, $1,500: one Crew bot, one workflow, 30 days. See AI bots for back-office work.
  • Private / local AI setup: if data needs to stay in-house. See private local AI.

Who it's for

  • Real estate teams juggling portal leads, CRM cleanup and transaction deadlines (AI for real estate teams)
  • Field, construction and home-services companies buried in intake, scheduling, quotes and invoices (AI for field and home-services companies)

About who does the work

I'm Collin Hill. I'm a Project Manager at TESCO and have spent the last 2–3 years building AI pipelines. I run a team of AI agents in my own work every day, so what I recommend is what I've tested on myself first. I do the audit myself; it isn't handed off.

AI Audit FAQs

How much does an AI audit cost?

At Embrose AI the AI Audit is $1,500, fixed price. That covers the workflow review, the ranked list of opportunities, tool recommendations and the written roadmap.

How long does the AI Audit take?

It depends on how many people and workflows are involved. We set the timeline on the free intro call, before you commit to anything.

Do you have to come on-site?

No. I do audits in person within about an hour of Franklin, including Greenwood, Indianapolis and Johnson County, or fully remote over video.

Do I have to hire you to build what the roadmap recommends?

No. The roadmap is yours. You can build it with me, with someone else, or on your own.

What if AI isn't a good fit for my business?

Then the roadmap will say so. I'd rather tell you that than sell you automations you don't need.

Will you need access to our systems?

Usually I just need to watch how the work is done. If we look inside a system, I ask for the minimum access needed and document it.
